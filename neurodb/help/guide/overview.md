# Getting around NeuroDB

NeuroDB brings together the programme monitoring data of the UNICEF Lebanon country office: eTools
(partners, programme documents, funds, assurance, field monitoring and action points), the country
programme, the Compiler figures of Makani, Dirasa and the youth programmes, population figures, a
library of studies and maps, and the documents people add. Every page reads NeuroDB's own copy of these
sources, refreshed on a schedule (see [When the data is refreshed](/help/overview/#when-the-data-is-refreshed)).

This guide explains how NeuroDB works: what a page shows, where a number comes from, what a rule
checks. Questions about the programme data themselves ("how many visits were off track in Akkar?")
are answered by [Ask NeuroDB](/ask/) and, for the visits of a filter, by Chat with Data in
[Monitoring insights](/fmm/).

## The top bar

- **Search** (Ctrl K, or the slash key): finds pages, partners, programme documents, country programme
  indicators, the documents of the Documents page and the archive of past monthly results (its
  databases, reports and indicators of every year, each with its year, marked as the archive: see [Past
  monthly results](/help/overview/#past-monthly-results)). It does not look
  into the KM Database, which is kept apart (its page is found under Pages). When Ask NeuroDB is switched on, the search box also offers
  to ask your words as a question; nothing is sent until you press Ask.
- **Year menu**: the calendar year the page shows, on pages that show one year; the archive's pages list
  its years; pages with a period of their own keep their own choice.
- The **NeuroDB logo** opens the page NeuroDB starts on: Ask NeuroDB while the AI is on, else Home.
- **Dark mode** switch, and your **user menu** (your role and section, Data health, sign out;
  Administration for administrators).
- **?**: opens the **Help assistant** (also Ctrl+Shift+H, or Cmd+Shift+H on a Mac). It answers how a
  page, rule, chart or number works, from this guide and from the live settings. Esc, the ×, or the
  shortcut again closes it. The shortcut also closes a quick view that is open, then opens the assistant.

## The sidebar

Most people see a short menu: Ask NeuroDB and Home at the top, then the pages of partner reporting and
field monitoring:

- **Ask NeuroDB** (marked AI): questions in plain language answered from the data, and the page NeuroDB
  opens first while the AI is on ([Ask NeuroDB](/help/ask-neurodb/)).
- **Home**, right under it: partner reporting in numbers and tables, and the page NeuroDB opens first when
  the AI is off ([Home](/help/home/)).
- **Partner reporting (Results)**: **Progress reports**, how partners report on their programme documents
  (due, submitted, accepted, sent back and overdue reports), and **Partner monitoring**, what each
  programme document indicator reached, with Program Performance one tab away
  ([Partner progress](/help/partner-progress/)).
- **Knowledge base**: **Field monitoring**, with the HACT visit plan
  ([Field monitoring and the HACT visit plan](/help/field-monitoring/)). A page of this group added to
  your menu (Monitoring insights, say) shows under the same title, in the full menu's order.

Superusers see the full menu below, and choose in the administration's *Menu* page what each role sees:
any page of the full menu can be added to a role's sidebar there. A page left out of your menu still opens
from the search, from links and from bookmarks: only the menu is shorter. Find a page by its name in the
search, and the Help pages from the **?** button (*Help pages*).

### The full menu

The full menu puts the pages of every day first and the archive last. It is the same for
every section:

- At the top, without a title: **Ask NeuroDB** (marked AI), questions in plain language answered from the
  data and the page NeuroDB opens first while the AI is on ([Ask NeuroDB](/help/ask-neurodb/)); **Home**,
  right under it ([Home](/help/home/)); **For you**, NeuroDB Watch, with the number of points that need
  you ([For you](/help/watch/)); **Daily review**, the morning check's findings
  ([Daily review](/help/briefs-and-overview/#the-daily-review)); and **What's new**, the changes NeuroDB
  noticed in any source ([What's new](/help/watch/#whats-new)).
- **Programme results**: **Country overview**, every figure of the year
  ([Country overview](/help/briefs-and-overview/#the-country-overview)); **Management brief**, the
  overview read for decisions ([Management brief](/help/briefs-and-overview/#the-management-brief));
  **Country programme**, outcomes, outputs, indicators and their progress, once a cycle is uploaded.
- **Knowledge base**: **Partner monitoring** (what each programme document indicator reached, by month
  and location), **Progress reports**, **Programme documents** and **Partners**
  ([Partner progress](/help/partner-progress/)), then **Funds**, **Donors**, **Assurance**, **Field
  monitoring** with the HACT visit plan ([Field monitoring and the HACT visit
  plan](/help/field-monitoring/)), **Monitoring insights** (marked AI while its AI is on;
  [Monitoring insights](/help/monitoring-insights/)) and **Action points**
  ([Action points](/help/action-points/)).
- **Education and youth**: **Makani (MSCC)**, **Makani wellbeing**, **Dirasa (Bridging)** and **Youth
  programmes**, once Compiler's figures are there.
- **Resources**: **Documents** (the reports, studies and evaluations people add), **Periodic reports**,
  **Document review** (marked AI, once it is on), **Library**, **KM Database** ([KM
  Database](/help/km-database/)), **Maps**, **Population** and **Data health**
  ([Documents](/help/knowledge-and-documents/)).
- At the bottom, the archive of past monthly results, closed until you open it ([Past monthly
  results](/help/overview/#past-monthly-results)). NeuroDB remembers whether you left it open or closed.
- **Help**: this guide.

A page that is not set up yet (the country programme, the Compiler pages, the document review) is left
out of the sidebar; administrators who can open the administration see it greyed, marked *Set up*,
and its page says how to set it up. A role whose menu has such a page added follows the same rule.

## Past monthly results

In past years partners reported their monthly results in another system; they now report progress in
eTools. Those past results are kept as they were, in the archive of past monthly results at the bottom of
the full menu, closed until you open it, with the years it holds. Inside are the databases of each year,
each with its dashboard, analytical view, snapshot and map, its indicators and their targets, and that
year's reports.

A partner's past records still open from their own address (a bookmark or an Ask NeuroDB answer), and the
search finds the archive's databases, reports and indicators, each marked as the archive. No other page
shows the archive or its figures: children reached, the management brief, Home, the donor pages and the
country programme count eTools partner reports alone. Ask NeuroDB answers from the archive only when you
ask about it.

**Who decides.** An administrator sets in the administration (under Reporting setup) whether the archive
counts as in use, as archived, or follows its import on its own (in use while the import runs and the year
has recent records). This changes only the archive's label and whether failures of its import are listed
for administrators; nothing is deleted.

## Links open in a quick view

Links open in a quick view; use **Open full page**, or Ctrl/Cmd-click to open the page in a new tab. A figure or a
name in a table opens its page in a window over the one you are on, with the same filter, so you see the rows behind a
number without leaving. Links in the window open in it, **Back** goes to the one before, and Esc or the × closes it.
A filter you change in the window keeps your place: you stay in the same field, what you type while it loads is kept,
and the filters you opened on a phone stay open. The menu, tabs, filters, sorting and downloads work as before, and
Ask NeuroDB and the printable reports always open as a page. A link in an Ask NeuroDB answer opens in the window too,
over your conversation; a page that cannot open there opens in a new tab, so the conversation stays.

## Pages that open at once

Home, the country overview, the daily review, the management brief, each partner's page, each programme
document's page (its open action points and timeline only), Data health, partner monitoring, the Map tab of Monitoring insights, the country programme, youth
programmes, the donor page and three views of the document review (*By document*, *Themes* and
*Repeated findings*) open at once and fill in as their figures are ready (the other pages are quick enough to come whole, as before):

- The frame comes first: the page's heading, filters and tabs, with a grey box where each block of
  figures will go, about the size of what will be there, so the page does not jump.
- Each box then fills in on its own, the ones you can see first and the ones further down soon
  after. You can read, scroll and change the filters meanwhile.
- A box still working after 8 seconds says "Still working on this part. Large filters take longer."
- A box that could not load says so in its place and has a **Try again** button; the rest of the page
  keeps working. If it fails again, reload the page; if it still fails, tell an administrator which
  page and which box.
- If the page itself could not start (its script was blocked or did not download), every box says it
  could not load and offers **Show the figures** instead: the same page with all its figures in it,
  which takes a little longer to come. The donor page says instead that its charts could not be
  drawn: reload it.
- A link to a block further down (a tab of the brief, a panel of a partner's page) waits for its box,
  then takes you there.
- On a partner's page, everything from the key figures to the last list fills in as one box; the
  Monitoring insights panel follows it, on partners a field monitoring visit has monitored.
- On partner monitoring, the tabs and the filters come at once. On Data quality, its tiles fill in
  first, then its lists, then the grid's key figures and list below them; on Program Performance, the
  section tiles, then the table. Changing a filter, a sort or a section tile refills the boxes under the
  filters. See [Partner progress](/help/partner-progress/).
- On Monitoring insights, the Map tab opened from a link or a bookmark shows the filters, key figures
  and tabs first, then the map and its tables. See [Monitoring insights](/help/monitoring-insights/).
- On the country programme page, the cycle, its documents and how much of it has gone come at once;
  the key figures and the outcomes at a glance fill in first, then each outcome with its indicators. A
  link to an outcome waits for its box, then takes you there. Opened again within 10 minutes, the page
  comes complete at once.
- On the youth programmes page, the filters come with the page; the key figures and the youth
  indicators fill in first, then the breakdowns and the two tables below them. The filters still apply
  when you press **Apply**, as before.
- On the donor page (the page donor accounts see, and administrators preview), the cards come at once
  in grey and the charts are drawn in them as soon as the figures arrive; if they cannot be loaded, a
  box says so with a **Try again** button.
- In the document review, *By document*, *Themes* and *Repeated findings* show their tabs, links and
  filters first, then the documents, the themes or the groups. See
  [Documents](/help/knowledge-and-documents/).
- The management brief's **Print** button waits for every box and its charts ("Preparing the
  page…"), then prints. Printing with Ctrl+P before a page is complete prints a short note in place of
  each box still loading: wait until the page is complete.
- The figures are the same as before: only the order in which they appear has changed. See
  [Home](/help/home/) and [Country overview, management brief and daily
  review](/help/briefs-and-overview/).

## Roles

Every signed-in person has one role:

| Role | What it adds |
|---|---|
| Viewer | Reads every page, asks Ask NeuroDB, the Help assistant and Chat with Data, and exports. Nothing is changed. |
| Section editor | Adds documents to the Documents page; marks the visits of their section reviewed; verifies the action points of their section; adds NeuroDB action points; writes the section comments of the HACT visit plan on the programme documents of their section. |
| Administrator | Everything a section editor does, for every section, plus the administration pages: settings, quality rules, prompt versions, scheduled jobs and the buttons that run them, and the card versions compared with Power BI. |

Every role sees the short menu of [The sidebar](/help/overview/#the-sidebar) unless a superuser chose
other pages for it in the administration's *Menu* page; superusers see the full menu. The menu only
changes what the sidebar lists: what a role may read or change is the table above.

Your **section** (set on your user by an administrator) is what several pages show first: Home, the
country overview, the daily review, Monitoring insights and the action points page open on your
section, with a chip or a filter to see every section. Members of the **Management** group (filled by administrators) also get
the whole-country view of For you. A **donor account** sees only its own donor page: none of the
pages in this guide, and not the Help assistant.

## Where the data comes from

| Source | What NeuroDB reads |
|---|---|
| eTools Datamart | Partners, programme documents, funds reservations and grants, PD indicators and the partners' progress reports, HACT assurance (assessments, audits, spot checks), TPM visits, field monitoring (the findings of each visit, the checklist answers, the programme activities) and action points. |
| eTools locations | The gazetteer: governorates, districts and cadasters with their P-codes. |
| Compiler | Makani and Dirasa figures, youth programme figures and the Makani wellbeing flags (counts only, never a child). |
| Country programme | The results framework and its progress, added by an administrator in the administration (Country programme cycles) and shown on the country programme page. |
| Documents and library | Documents people add, library publications and country programme documents. |
| KM Database | The evidence workbook an administrator uploads in the administration (KM Database → Versions): it changes only when a new version is uploaded, and only the KM Database page shows it ([KM Database](/help/km-database/)). |

## Why NeuroDB's figures may differ from Power BI

Power BI's Makani, Dirasa and Youth dashboards read three saved queries ("views") of Compiler's database:
`vw_mscc_data`, `vw_bridging_data` and `vw_youth_data_all`. Compiler now counts the same registrations as
those views for NeuroDB, and a line under the key figures of the Makani, Dirasa and youth pages says how
the figures were counted:

- **"Counted from Power BI's view …"**: the same registrations Power BI reads (deleted ones left out), with
  the time Compiler counted them.
- **"Power BI's view could not be used (reason) …"**: Compiler counted with its own rule, so the figures
  may differ from Power BI. The reason says what is missing (often Compiler's right to read the view, which
  an administrator of Compiler's database gives).
- **"Not yet compared with Power BI's view"**: the figures came from Compiler before this comparison
  existed; the next night's count brings it.

Past years are counted again too (once a week), so a past year no longer keeps the figures counted while
it was the current year. Even on the same registrations, a card can still differ, for three reasons:

- **The time of the count**: Compiler counts at night, Power BI at its own refresh. Compare them right
  after Power BI's refresh.
- **What a card counts**: Power BI's exact formula is not always known (each child once or each
  registration, blank answers counted or not, which health question, which list of staff or teachers).
  NeuroDB counts every possible version; **Data health → Compared with Power BI** shows each version's
  number, and an administrator chooses the one that matches (below).
- **"Not specified"**: the percentage charts leave blank answers out, as Power BI's do; the key figures
  still count those registrations.

Dirasa's *Children enrolled* card counts each child once, which is what Power BI's card appears to count,
with the number of registrations under it. A child is counted once only under the filters Compiler groups
the children by: no filter; any one of partner, centre or school, gender, nationality, CWD type, Makani's
governorate or Dirasa's round; the partner with one more of these; on Makani the governorate, or partner and
governorate together, alone or with one more of these; and gender with nationality, CWD type or the round.
Dirasa's governorate filter is the school's, so it never has this count. Under other filters Dirasa's card
shows the registrations and says so, and Makani's *Children enrolled (unique count)* shows "—". The youth
page has **All years**, which also counts the registrations saved before September 2025 without a year.

**Dirasa by year.** Like Power BI's page "Dirasa Programme Dashboard 2026", the Dirasa page shows a year:
the Compiler rounds whose Bridging start date is in that year (a round without one counts in the year
its name gives), or, if Compiler is set to count the rounds marked current, those rounds (the line under
the title says which). That line lists the rounds and their registrations. When Compiler counted Power
BI's view, the view may give the year some registrations of a round of another year: the line names such a
round apart, after the year's own ("also counted, from rounds of another year: Bridging 2025-2026 (2025, 12
registrations)"), never as a round of the year, and may add "other rounds or no round" for registrations
whose round it does not list, or without one. The **Round** filter shows one of the rounds that have
registrations or teachers, a round of another year after the year's own and with its year ("Bridging
2025-2026 (round of 2025)": only its registrations counted in the year); a link to any other round shows
the whole year and says so. A child registered in two rounds of the year counts once in *Children
enrolled* and twice in the registrations.

**Rounds in no year.** A Compiler round with neither a start date nor a year in its name (Makani: nor a
year) belongs to no year, so its registrations are counted in none of the years shown, while Power BI's
view may count them: **Data health → Compared with Power BI** says how many and in which rounds, under
Makani's or Dirasa's table. Giving the round a start date in Compiler places it in a year at the next
count.

**Choosing a card's version** (administrators): open Administration → **Power BI cards**, open the card,
type the number Power BI shows for it, choose its period in the list (the periods Data health compares: for
Dirasa the year, such as 2026 (in counts made before Compiler counted Dirasa by year, the round's name); left
empty, the latest period, the one the page opens on), and save. Data health → *Compared
with Power BI* then marks the version that gives that number ("equals Power BI"); choose that version in
the same card and save. The pages, and the country programme's children enrolled, show it at once. A card
whose versions all differ from Power BI's number says "No version equals Power BI's number": tell the
NeuroDB team. The version marked "default" is the one shown until an administrator chooses another; it is
the card's count before this change except for Dirasa's children (before: "Registrations") and in-school
children and CWD (now the schools with registrations only). **Power BI views** shows the
views' definitions, with names and lists of numbers hidden, to administrators only.

## When the data is refreshed

All times are Beirut time. Administrators can change a schedule; the Help assistant reads the
schedules as they are now.

| What | When |
|---|---|
| eTools Datamart sync | Every day at 20:30 |
| eTools locations | Every day at 05:00 |
| Monitoring insights refresh (visits, rules, scores, urgency) | Every day at 05:25, and after every Datamart sync |
| AI monitoring briefs | Every day at 05:40 |
| AI checks of the quality rules | Every day at 05:50 |
| Daily review | Every day at 06:00 |
| AI review of completed action points | Every day at 06:10 |
| Knowledge hub (and new library and country programme documents) | Every day at 07:00, and after every sync |
| What's new note | Every day at 07:30 |
| NeuroDB Watch (For you) | Every day at 07:45, and shortly after new data arrives |
| Compiler figures (youth, education, Makani wellbeing) | Every night, once Compiler is set up |
| Machine learning readiness check (Data health) | The 1st of each month at 06:45 |

The **Data health** page (user menu, or *Resources* in the full menu) shows when each source last synced and which
runs failed. It opens at once (see [Pages that open at once](/help/overview/#pages-that-open-at-once)):
the sync jobs fill in first, then the latest runs, then the eTools
datasets, each saying when the Datamart's answer was limited and how NeuroDB read it whole (or removed
nothing), *Compared with Power BI* and the machine learning readiness check. Pages show the date of their data next to every status ("On track · reported for Jun
2026", "status as of 3 Oct 2026").

## Ready for machine learning?

At the bottom of **Data health**, NeuroDB checks whether its data could support machine learning. It only
counts. Nothing is predicted, and nothing leaves NeuroDB. No names and no written text are used: counts, dates
and statuses only. By default the check runs by itself on the 1st of each month. The page says when it runs
next, or that its schedule is switched off. An administrator can also run it at any time: *Run a job → Machine
learning readiness*.

It measures:

- **What each source holds**: programme documents and their indicators, partner reports, assurance, the daily
  review, and population and Compiler figures.
- **How the sources link up**: for each source, the share of its rows linked to a programme document, a partner
  or an audit. For field monitoring it also says how the programme document was found (the same reference, the
  same PCA and PD numbers, the same number without its amendment, or the same title among the partner's PDs).
- **What arrives each year**: partner reports sent, by type; audits, special audits, spot checks and
  micro-assessments; field monitoring visits and findings; TPM visits; action points raised and completed;
  and PME verifications. Each is counted the way its own page counts it. The current year reads "so far".
- **Accepted values**: how many values a typical running PD indicator has in reports UNICEF accepted, and how
  many quarters they cover.
- **The action point history**: how many days of each action point's status NeuroDB has kept, counted from
  its first day (see [Status history](/help/action-points/#status-history)). It reads *Not kept yet* until
  NeuroDB keeps it.

Each question gets a verdict:

- **Ready**: every check passes.
- **Partly ready**: the main checks pass, but not all of them.
- **Not yet**: a main check fails, or most checks do.

The two cards are decisions a model could support; each says whether the data is ready for it now. **Analyses for later** lists six analyses the programme
may want one day: unusual results compared with similar programme documents (those that serve the same country
programme output); problems that keep coming back in field monitoring and audits; how results change over time;
which follow-ups get done and stay done; whether results change after a visit or an audit; and what a result
costs. Open one to see its checks and why it is not ready yet. When the data's pace allows it, it also says
about when it could be ready. Nothing is built yet: a verdict only says whether the data would be enough.

What a result costs always reads *Not yet*: NeuroDB holds no spending per programme document. Funds reserved
and disbursed are not spending. They show money set aside and paid to the partner, not what it was spent on.

**Since the last check** lists what changed since the check before: the main counts, and any verdict that
moved. Read it after each monthly check to see progress. When one of the two checks could not measure a
source, it says so, and a verdict that rests on that source is not compared.
